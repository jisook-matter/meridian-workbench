        WeatherNext 3 forecast feed

        sample data, published format

        Rain forecast for the storm hour, 15:00 to 16:00, as each hourly run arrives. One dot per ensemble member, bar from 10th to 90th percentile, line at the median. Values in mm.

        1. Pull each hourly run for the 0.1 degree cell over the catchment, all 64 members.
        2. Convert total_precipitation_1hr from metres to millimetres.
        3. Take ensemble statistics for each valid hour. These weight the experts hours ahead.
        4. Break the hourly total into 5-minute intensities with a storm temporal pattern, because a 10 km hourly grid cannot resolve a street-scale burst.
        5. Hand over to pit sensors and the 15-minute nowcast once rain starts.

Checked on 14 Nov 2026: it answers without a key and serves the current reading and hourly history for each station. The three Monash stations checked hold records from 1996. No Melbourne Water readings are stored on this page.

            Base address: api.melbournewater.com.au/rainfall-river-level. Dates are passed as fromDate and toDate. A history request for February 2011 returned the same Rowville peak the plan lists.

            Before it can be switched on

            • Consent. Melbourne Water's site terms require written consent to reuse its content. The API is undocumented and carries no licence. Meridian needs a data agreement first.
            • Server-side pull. A page like this one cannot call outside services. Meridian would poll the API hourly from its own service and keep each station's fault flag.
            • Unverified and hourly. Melbourne Water states the data is not checked. Hourly readings suit creek and basin levels. They are too coarse for flash flooding in the drains, which is what the pit sensors measure.
            • Bureau channel. The Bureau of Meteorology says it is working with Melbourne Water to publish this data on the Bureau's site, which may become the supported source.

            What Meridian adds

            • The model listens. Pit sensor readings correct the model state every minute, and a persistent gap between reading and model is treated as evidence that the network has changed.
            • Routing ahead of the storm. A gating layer weights eight specialist experts from each hourly forecast run, so storm experts take over hours before any sensor moves. Expert gating and anticipatory routing are the subject of Matter IO patent filings.
            • A rolling nowcast. Every five minutes the corrected model runs 15 minutes ahead, which is what drafts the alert early.
            • A named human releases the alert. Agents draft. An officer co-signs. Every step lands in a timestamped record.
            • Learning across sites. The blockage expert is designed for federated learning, where sites share model weight updates and raw data stays local. Not simulated here.

            What is real and what is not

            • Real: the Estate tab. 308 active City of Monash pits with coordinates, asset type, pit depth, fill level and debris level, read from the Matter platform on 14 Nov 2026.
            • From the 2019 plan: Local layers, Schematics, Severe weather and VICSES. 825 property entries, 135 council road closure streets, 19 VicRoads closure points, six Melbourne Water retarding basins, 49 flood events, 28 peak travel times, ten intelligence cards and 114 months of storm requests, read from the City of Monash Flood Emergency Plan v5.0. The plan carries its own disclaimers and may be out of date. Streets are placed by OpenStreetMap geocoding (© OpenStreetMap contributors), so markers sit on the street and not on the house. A few basin and gauge positions are estimated and marked approximate.
            • Not held: camera images at the basins, Melbourne Water live readings, and flood-extent mapping. Each is shown as a gap.
            • Stand-in: the catchment model. The platform holds no terrain or pipe data, so the model still runs on an illustrative creek valley. It follows the pattern in the VICSES flood guide for Monash: drains overflowing in intense rain, worst in low-lying areas.
            • To close the gap: a terrain grid, the pipe list with capacities, and the platform's recorded pit levels through one storm.

            Taking WeatherNext 3 live

            • Data is served through BigQuery, Earth Engine and Cloud Storage and needs Google allowlist access. A browser page cannot query it, so Meridian needs a server-side pull each hour.
            • Still to confirm with access in hand: table and field names beyond the documented variable, and how long after initialisation a run is available. This replay assumes three hours.
            • Google describes the forecasts as experimental and not official warnings. Here they stage crews and re-weight experts. A public alert is drafted only on sensor evidence and the nowcast, then co-signed.

            Limits of this prototype

            In the catchment model the terrain, storm depths, sensor readings and forecast are synthetic. The solver is a simplified local-inertial scheme and the pipe network is routed by capacity, so results show mechanism and workflow. They are not calibrated or validated. A production engine would wrap established solvers and needs sign-off by a qualified hydraulic engineer before any design-grade use.
